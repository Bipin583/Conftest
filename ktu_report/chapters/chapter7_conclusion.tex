\chapter{Conclusion and Future Scope}

\section{Summary of Contributions}
In this project, we designed, implemented, and empirically validated \textbf{ConfTest}, an uncertainty-aware, confidence-calibrated Selective Regression Test Selection framework. ConfTest combines machine-learning efficiency with a conservative full-suite fallback policy by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Mining a canonical 32-feature dataset covering AST, Call-Graph, Churn, and Historical telemetry.
    \item Quantifying epistemic uncertainty ($\sigma$) using 5-seed deep ensembles.
    \item Calibrating failure probabilities via Temperature Scaling at $T^* = 0.7941$, reducing validation ECE from $0.0390$ to $0.0242$, and rejecting isotonic regression because it nearly doubles worst-bin error even while lowering mean ECE.
    \item Enforcing a selective policy that observed $100.0\%$ failure recall with \emph{zero} escaped commits on $183$ held-out commits; this is an empirical result, not a formal guarantee for future commits or unseen repositories.
    \item Implementing a research-prototype service stack with FastAPI, Streamlit, and GitHub PR bot webhooks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{An Honest Statement of What Was and Was Not Achieved}
The shipped operating point met the empirical failure-recall objective on the held-out sample; the economic objective was not met. Neither result is a formal guarantee beyond that experiment.

At the shipped operating point the policy escaped zero regressions across $183$ held-out commits, because
it declines to select whenever ensemble disagreement or distributional novelty exceeds a tuned threshold.
That is a real, measured property. It also abstains on $97.81\%$ of commits, which means it reduces test
executions by $3.11\%$ and wall-clock time by $0.0\%$: the projected annual saving is $\$0$, not the
figure earlier drafts of this report carried. Tightening the threshold to reach a $32.85\%$ reduction
drops held-out failure recall to $87.69\%$ with $15$ escaped commits, missing the $95\%$ recall floor this
project pre-registered, with an $8.33$ percentage-point gap between the validation and held-out splits.

The binding constraint is the accuracy of the underlying ranker --- PR-AUC $0.1393$ against a $3.71\%$
positive rate, roughly four times a no-skill baseline but far from sufficient --- and not the
selective-prediction machinery built around it. Three specific weaknesses are documented rather than
omitted: $13$ of the $32$ canonical features are constant in training (including all $12$ diff-churn
columns, which indicates a diff-mining defect); zero-shot cross-repository transfer does not work
(macro recall $51.57\%$, below chance on one project); and every committed artifact predates the
correction of a LightGBM row-bagging misconfiguration that made the ensemble less diverse than intended.

We regard this as a useful negative result. The abstention gate is a reusable component for any future
RTS ranker strong enough to exploit it, and reporting the measured frontier is more valuable than
selecting a threshold on the test split in order to publish a larger number.

\section{Future Scope}
Future extensions of this work include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Multi-Language Support (Java, TypeScript, Go).
    \item Neural Graph Attention Networks (GATs) for deep whole-program call-graph embeddings.
    \item Multi-Armed Bandit dynamic test budget scheduling in resource-constrained edge CI environments.
\end{itemize}
